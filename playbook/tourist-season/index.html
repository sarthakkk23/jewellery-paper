Question 8 of 8 · The tourist season

"Foreign buyers fill the shop in winter and vanish by June. How do I plan the year around them?"

The short answer

November brings about six times June's foreign visits. Order winter stock in September, and use summer for the work winter crowds out.

My view: the foreign season is steep and very regular, so it can be planned for months ahead. The local calendar runs differently, which makes the quiet foreign months useful rather than empty.

The figure. Over 2023–2025, November carried 14.3% of Rajasthan's foreign tourist visits and June 2.4%, about a sixth as much [Measured: Rajasthan Tourism progress reports; Arithmetic]. January–March and November–December together carried 62% over the three years (64% in 2025 alone) [Arithmetic].

Caveats.

  • These are visits, not visitors: one person who sees Jaipur and Udaipur counts twice.
  • They're for Rajasthan, not Jaipur. Jaipur's share of the state's foreign visits isn't settled yet, so this page uses only the shape of the year.
  • Domestic visits are far more numerous: about 130 for every foreign visit in 2025 [Arithmetic]. They peak at different times (July, September, December), for reasons this data doesn't show.
  • Foreign visits in 2025 were 6% below 2024 and 21% above 2019 [Measured; Arithmetic]. The shape of the year has held; the size moves.

A worked example: an old-city shop that sells to foreign visitors

The shop sells ₹24 lakh a year to foreign visitors [Example], and its sales follow the state's foreign visits month by month [Example: a simplification; the calculator lets you use your own months]. It likes to hold about two months of expected sales as stock [Example], and its karigars need about six weeks to make a batch [Example].

Every step of the sum 4 steps

01 · Arithmetic. What each month brings.

  • November: ₹24 lakh × 14.33% (the unrounded share) = ₹3.44 lakh.
  • June: ₹24 lakh × 2.40% = ₹0.58 lakh.
  • November–March (five months): ₹14.9 lakh. May–September (five months): ₹5.2 lakh.

02 · Arithmetic. The stock each season needs.

  • Going into November (two months' cover, November and December): ₹3.440 + ₹2.643 = ₹6.08 lakh.
  • Going into June (June and July): ₹0.576 + ₹0.918 = ₹1.49 lakh, about a quarter of the November figure.

03 · Arithmetic. When to order.

  • To have November's stock in the shop by 1 November, with a six-week making time, the order goes to the karigars by about 20 September.
  • September carries 5.8% of foreign visits, so the order goes in during one of the quiet months. That's the useful part: the making happens while the shop has time for it.

04 · Judgement. What I'd do.

My view:

  • Treat late September as the start of the winter season, not November. That's when the stock decisions get made.
  • Use May to September for what winter leaves no time for: making stock, repairs, training a new hand, photographing pieces, and local customers, whose calendar runs differently.
  • Keep summer stock lean, but not empty. A quiet month still brings 2.4% to 6.4% of the year's foreign visits.
  • Write down your own month-by-month sales for one year. The state's curve is a starting point; your shop's own curve is the one to plan on.

The formula
  • month_sales(m) = annual × share(m)
  • stock_needed(m) = Σ month_sales over the next k months, starting at m
  • order_by(m) = first day of m − lead time

What to do this week

  1. Pull last year's sales to foreign customers by month from your books, even roughly. Compare them with the state's curve above.
  2. Put an order-by date in the calendar for next winter's stock: six weeks (or your karigar's real lead time) before 1 November.
  3. Pick one thing for the next quiet season that winter never leaves time for, and write it down now.
