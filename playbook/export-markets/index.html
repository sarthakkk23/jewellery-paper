Question 6 of 8 · Export markets beyond the US

"The US got harder. Where else can I sell, and what duty will my buyer pay?"

The short answer

The UAE, UK, Australia, EFTA and Oman now take Indian jewellery at zero duty. Pick one and learn its paperwork.

My view: several big markets now let Indian jewellery in at zero duty. Exporters are already growing in the UAE and Australia, and the UK opened in July 2026.

The figure. India's gem and jewellery exports to the US fell 44.42% in April–December 2025 [Measured: GJEPC data via Indian Jeweller, 19 Jan 2026].

Who's already moving [Measured: GJEPC FY2025-26 via Tribune/ANI, 15 Apr 2026]:

  • Exports to the UAE reached US$8.70 bn (+10.52%).
  • Silver jewellery exports rose 52.21% to US$1.47 bn.
  • Note: the same report gives Australia +38.33%, while GJEPC's own Solitaire (22 Aug 2026) gives +16% to US$374 m. The two may measure different things. Check before quoting either.

A worked example: one silver pendant, four markets

A Jaipur exporter sells a 925 silver pendant at US$40 FOB, with US$1.50 freight to the buyer [Example].

01 · Measured. The duty rates (table above). For the US I use only the 10% Section 301 duty. Any normal US duty on the line comes on top, so the real US figure is higher [Estimate, set against the conclusion: it makes the US look better than it is].

04 · Judgement. What I'd do.

My view:

  • Don't drop US buyers who still order. Do pick one zero-duty market to build properly this year, not five half-heartedly.
  • The UK is newly open, and the EU is expected next.
  • Zero duty under an FTA usually needs proof that the goods qualify as Indian (rules of origin and a certificate). Ask GJEPC or DGFT before you quote a buyer "duty-free".

Rules that matter here

Rules that matter here
  • RCMC: under Foreign Trade Policy para 2.57(a), an applicant for an authorisation or any other FTP benefit "shall be required to provide, the RCMC" (DGFT, FTP Chapter 2: content.dgft.gov.in/…/Chapter_2_Revised_FTP_v1.2.2.pdf, opened). Which council issues it for jewellery is set in Appendix 2T, which I didn't open.
  • Gems and jewellery schemes: FTP Chapter 4, paras 4.30–4.53, "Schemes for exporters of gems and jewellery", covers replenishment authorisations, nominated agencies, and export against supply by a foreign buyer (content.dgft.gov.in/…/Chapter_4_Revised_FTP_2026.pdf, opened).
  • For any shipment, check the tariff line and origin rules with GJEPC, DGFT or your customs broker.

The formula
  • landed = FOB × (1 + d) + freight
  • gap vs cheapest = landed − min(landed)
  • gap % = gap ÷ min(landed)

What to do this week

  1. Pick one market from the table where you already have a contact, a relative or a past enquiry.
  2. Ask GJEPC's Jaipur office what the certificate of origin process is for that market.
  3. Go and look: the Jaipur Silver Association's JSA Silver Show runs 22–24 November 2026 (Indian Jeweller, 26 Oct 2026). It's a cheap way to see which buyers are in town.
