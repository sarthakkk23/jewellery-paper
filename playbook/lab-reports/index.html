Question 7 of 8 · Lab reports for coloured stones

"Buyers keep asking for a lab report. When is it worth certifying a stone?"

The short answer

When the fee is a small share of the stone's value. Ask the buyer which lab first.

My view: a report pays for itself once it lifts the price by more than its own cost. For stones above a certain value that's a low bar. For small stones it rarely is, and lot reports may fit better.

The figure. Commercial-quality Zambian emerald rough averaged US$9.72 a carat at Gemfields' August–September 2026 auction, more than double two years earlier [Measured: Gemfields RNS, 11 Sep 2026]. Dearer rough raises the stakes on every finished stone, and on every buyer's doubt about it.

What's available in Jaipur

  • IIGJ's Jaipur lab (GJEPC's former Gem Testing Laboratory) now issues origin reports for packet lots and bead strings of ruby, emerald and sapphire (Indian Jeweller, 12 Mar 2026). It also has a collection centre at the Jewellers Association's premises in Johari Bazar (GJEPC Solitaire).
  • IGI opened a second Jaipur lab, in Sitapura, on 29 Jan 2026, with origin and treatment detection (IGI).
  • GSI issues colour trade-name reports (such as "pigeon blood") in Jaipur (Indian Jeweller, 19 Aug 2026).
  • I found no published Jaipur price list. The one published fee I could read is GIA India's (labs in Mumbai and Surat): an Identification Report for 0.01–1.99 ct at ₹6,705, effective 1 Jan 2026 [Measured, with a caveat: the PDF's layout was garbled, so confirm the column against GIA's schedule].

A worked example: when does a ₹6,705 report pay?

A cutter is deciding whether to certify single emeralds of different values [Example values]. Fee: ₹6,705 [Measured, GIA India; GST not stated].

02 · Judgement. Reading the table.

My view:

  • Above about ₹1 lakh, a report needs only a modest lift to pay, and often the lift is the sale itself: no report, no deal.
  • Below about ₹50,000, a single-stone report eats the margin.
  • For small stones, a lot or strand report (now offered in Jaipur) spreads one fee over many stones. Ask your lab for its lot price.

03 · Judgement. What I'd do.

My view:

  • Ask buyers which lab they want before you certify. A report from the wrong lab is a fee for nothing.
  • Get a written price list from two Jaipur labs. Then the calculator below runs on real numbers, not mine.

What to do this week

  1. Ask your last five buyers which lab's report they'd accept, and write it down.
  2. Get written fee lists from two labs in Jaipur (IIGJ's lab and IGI both have Sitapura addresses). Ask specifically for lot and strand prices.
  3. Run your next five stones through the calculator before you send them.
