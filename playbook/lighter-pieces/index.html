Question 2 of 8 · Lighter and lower-carat pieces

"Customers want the same look for less. Do I make it lighter, or in 18-carat?"

The short answer

₹2 lakh buys 13.4 g of 22K or 16.3 g of 18K. Show customers both and let them choose.

My view: buyers are already doing this sum. On the same budget, 18-carat gives 22% more weight to work with [Arithmetic, step 03]. Lighter 22-carat keeps the purity but shrinks the piece.

The figure.

  • India's jewellery demand in April–June 2026 fell 15.4% by weight, to 75.1 tonnes, but rose 34.4% by value [Measured: World Gold Council, via Indian Jeweller, 27 Aug 2026].
  • Buyers are moving to lighter and lower-carat pieces (same source).

A worked example: a ₹2 lakh budget

A customer comes in with ₹2,00,000 to spend, tax included [Example]. The shop charges 12% making on all purities [Example].

Every step of the sum 6 steps

01 · Measured and Estimate. The rates.

  • 22K: ₹12,980.6 a gram [Measured, IBJA 916].
  • 18K: ₹10,638.9 a gram [Estimate, purity ratio from IBJA 999].

02 · Arithmetic. All-in cost per gram (gold + 12% making + 3% GST).

  • 22K: ₹12,980.6 × 1.12 × 1.03 = ₹14,974 a gram.
  • 18K: ₹10,638.9 × 1.12 × 1.03 = ₹12,273 a gram.

03 · Arithmetic. What ₹2 lakh buys.

  • 22K: 2,00,000 ÷ 14,974 = 13.36 g.
  • 18K: 2,00,000 ÷ 12,273 = 16.30 g, which is 22% more metal weight to work with (less of it is pure gold, of course).

04 · Arithmetic. The same design in 18K.

  • A 13.36 g design costs 2,00,000 × 10,638.9 ÷ 12,980.6 = ₹1,63,920 in 18K (the same as 13.36 g × ₹12,273, before rounding the grams).
  • That's ₹36,080 (18%) less than in 22K.

05 · Arithmetic. And a year ago?

  • At last year's 22K rate [Estimate], ₹2 lakh bought 15.68 g.
  • Today it buys 13.36 g: 14.8% less for the same money.
  • That's close to the national fall in weight, though WGC's figure covers a different period.

06 · Judgement. What I'd do.

My view:

  • Don't push anyone off 22K. For many families, purity is the point, especially for wedding gold and anything bought as savings.
  • Offer a choice at the counter: the same design in 22K and 18K, side by side, priced.
  • Keep a lighter 22K range for customers who want the purity.
  • Let the customer choose. Your job is to make the trade-off visible, not to decide it for them.

What to do this week

  1. Pick your three best-selling designs and price each in 22K and 18K on one card.
  2. Ask your karigar which of them can be made lighter without looking thin: a hollower back, a finer chain, fewer stones.
  3. Show both prices to the next ten customers who hesitate, and note which they choose. Ten answers will tell you more than any report.

Rules that matter here

Rules that matter here
  • Hallmarking grades: BIS hallmarking now spans 9K to 24K, including 18K (Indian Jeweller, 24 Jul 2025, reporting BIS's Amendment No. 2, effective July 2025: www.indianjeweller.in/…/india-extends-mandatory-hallmarking-to-include-9-carat-gold, opened). Every grade you sell in Jaipur must be hallmarked with HUID unless the piece or the business is exempt (see page 4). Check the current grade list with BIS or your hallmarking centre.
