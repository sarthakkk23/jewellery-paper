Question 1 of 8 · Making charges

"Gold went up again. Do I raise making charges, or absorb it?"

The short answer

Know what your work costs in rupees per gram; then you can see what a gold rise does to the bill, whichever way you charge.

My view: the gold itself can't be absorbed. It's about 89% of the price before GST [Arithmetic: 1 ÷ 1.12]. The part that's yours to decide is the making charge, and there are two common ways to set it: as a percentage of the gold, or in rupees. Each behaves differently when gold moves.

The figure. IBJA's 999 rate went from ₹1,20,670 per 10 g (6 Nov 2025) to ₹1,41,710 (26 Oct 2026) [Measured]. That's +17.4% in under a year [Arithmetic].

A worked example: one 22-carat necklace, a year apart

An invented family shop in the old city sells a 20 g, 22-carat necklace with making charged at 12% of the gold value. [Example: 20 g, 12%. Published ranges for making charges run from about 3% to 25% of gold value (BankBazaar, updated 26 Oct 2026; IIFL, 24 May 2024) [Estimate, sourced].]

Every step of the sum 5 steps

01 · Measured. What the gold costs.

  • A year ago: 22K at ₹1,10,533 per 10 g [Estimate, see the table above] → ₹11,053.3 a gram.
  • Now: ₹1,29,806 per 10 g [Measured] → ₹12,980.6 a gram.

02 · Arithmetic. The bill, then and now.

A year agoNow
Gold: 20 g × rate₹2,21,067₹2,59,612
Making: 12% of gold₹26,528₹31,153
GST: 3% of (gold + making)₹7,428₹8,723
Customer pays₹2,55,023₹2,99,488

GST is 3% of the whole value, making included, "whether the making charge is shown separately or not" (CBEC FAQ, Gems & Jewellery, Q7; see Rules).

03 · Arithmetic. Where the rise went.

  • The necklace costs ₹44,465 more [Arithmetic].
  • ₹38,545 of that is gold [Arithmetic].
  • ₹4,625 is making, for exactly the same work [Arithmetic].

With a percentage making charge, making moves with gold: here it rose 17.4%, the same as the gold rate, while the work on the piece stayed the same. Per gram, making went from ₹1,326 to ₹1,558 [Arithmetic].

04 · Arithmetic. The same necklace with making held in rupees.

  • Making stays at last year's ₹26,528. That's 10.2% of today's gold value [Arithmetic].
  • The customer pays ₹2,94,724, which is ₹4,764 less, or 1.6% off the bill [Arithmetic].
  • The shop's making income is the same as last year's.

05 · Judgement. One way to look at it.

My view:

  • Both ways are common, and both work. A percentage is simple and familiar to customers. Rupees per gram ties the charge to the work itself.
  • It helps to know what making covers in rupees per gram: karigar, wastage, finishing, a share of the shop. With that number in hand, either method can be checked against it.
  • In a year when gold rises fast, the percentage method moves making up with it. That extra ₹4,625 is also the part of the bill customers most often bargain over, so some shops choose to hold making in rupees and say so: "making at last year's rate". It costs the shop nothing it was earning before.
  • When costs do rise (karigar wages, rent), the rupee figure can rise by that much, with a reason customers can understand.

The formula
  • gold_now = w × r_now; gold_then = w × r_then
  • making_now = m × gold_now; making_then = m × gold_then
  • total_now = (gold_now + making_now) × (1 + g)
  • held_total = (gold_now + making_then) × (1 + g)
  • held_pct = making_then ÷ gold_now
  • saving = total_now − held_total

What to do this week

  1. Cost your five best-selling designs in rupees per gram: karigar, wastage and finishing. Write the number on the card you keep for each design.
  2. Compare it with what your current making charge comes to at today's rate. Where it sits well above the rupee cost, there's room for an offer if you want one.
  3. If you'd like to test it, try one line on the counter for a fortnight ("Making at last year's rate on these designs") and count how many customers ask about it.

Rules that matter here

Rules that matter here
  • GST: "GST is payable at the rate of 3% of the total transaction value of jewellery", whether making is shown separately or not (CBEC, Sectoral Series: Gems & Jewellery FAQ, Q7). A registered job worker pays "5% on job charges only" (Q8). gstcouncil.gov.in/…/sectoral-fq-gems-jewellery.pdf. This is a 2017 FAQ, not a notification. The 3% rate for jewellery was kept unchanged in September 2025 (Business Standard, 4 Sep 2025). Check with your CA.
