Question 4 of 8 · Hallmarking and HUID

"Hallmarking just went up to ₹75 a piece. What does it really cost me, and what's exempt?"

The short answer

Well under 1% of a typical gold piece. For silver it's closer to 1%, and it's your choice.

My view: for gold, the new fee is well under 1% of a typical piece. It matters most for very light gold and for silver, where you're in charge of whether to hallmark at all.

The figure.

  • From 14 September 2026 the BIS hallmarking charge for gold is ₹75 per article, minimum ₹200 per consignment, up from ₹45 [Measured: BIS (Hallmarking) Amendment Regulations 2026, reported by News on AIR, 16 Sep 2026].
  • Silver stays at ₹35 per article, minimum ₹150 (same source).

What the rules say (quoted from BIS)

  • Jaipur has been covered by mandatory gold hallmarking since 23 June 2021 (phase 1) [Measured: BIS phase-wise district list, Sep 2026].
  • Marks: a "Six digit HUID number" is a mandatory part of the hallmark. "mentioning of HUID numbers on invoice is voluntary at present" (BIS, Hallmarking – Jewellers page).
  • Since 1 April 2023, "the sale of only gold jewellery with HUID shall be permitted" (government statement reported by PTI, 4 Mar 2023).
  • Exempt (BIS Jewellers page):
    • jewellers with "annual turnover of upto Rs.40 lakh";
    • "an article with weight less than two grams";
    • "special categories of jewellery -Kundan, Polki and Jadaau";
    • "any article meant for export";
    • articles for government-approved B2B exhibitions.
  • Registration: "No fee is being charged from jewellers" (same page).
  • Silver: HUID-based hallmarking has been voluntary since 1 September 2025 under IS 2112:2025, in grades 800, 835, 925, 958, 970, 990 and 999 (PTI via The Week, 4 Sep 2025; GJEPC Solitaire, 5 Sep 2025).
  • Tax on the fee: "Applicable taxes will continue to be levied separately" (SCC Online on the 2026 regulations). The rate isn't stated in an official text I could read. An advance-ruling digest applies 18%; I use 18% below as an Estimate, which is set high, against this page's "it's small" conclusion.
  • The exemptions have conditions. Check your own case with BIS (bis.gov.in) or your hallmarking centre, and the tax with your CA.

A worked example: a family shop's month

Each week the shop sends one consignment of 30 pieces and one of 2 rush pieces [Example].

Every step of the sum 5 steps

01 · Arithmetic. Per consignment, at ₹75 and 18% tax.

  • 30 pieces: 30 × ₹75 = ₹2,250 (above the ₹200 minimum) → ₹2,655 with tax → ₹88.5 a piece.
  • 2 pieces: 2 × ₹75 = ₹150, below the minimum, so ₹200 applies → ₹236 with tax → ₹118 a piece.

02 · Arithmetic. What changed from ₹45.

  • The 30-piece lot rose from ₹1,593 to ₹2,655, i.e. +₹1,062 a week.
  • The 2-piece lot is ₹200 either way: 2 × ₹45 = ₹90 and 2 × ₹75 = ₹150 are both under the ₹200 minimum. I've assumed the same minimum applied before the change [Estimate: the AIR report doesn't state the old minimum].
  • Over a month (4 of each): ₹11,564, against ₹7,316 before, i.e. +₹4,248 [Arithmetic; the 'before' figure uses the assumed old minimum].

03 · Arithmetic. As a share of the piece.

  • An 8 g 22K ring at 12% making sells for about ₹1,19,795. ₹88.5 is 0.07% of it.
  • A 2.5 g earring sent in a rush lot sells for about ₹37,436. ₹118 is 0.32% of it.

04 · Arithmetic. Silver, where it's your choice.

  • A 25 g sterling (925) pendant holds about ₹4,497 of silver (25 × 0.925 × ₹194.479/g).
  • Hallmarked in a 30-piece lot at ₹35 + 18%, it costs ₹41.3, which is 0.92% of the silver value.
  • On the same basis (metal value only), the ring's fee is 0.085% (₹88.5 ÷ ₹1,03,845 of gold) [Arithmetic]. Silver's share is about eleven times gold's. For silver, the question is whether your buyers will pay for the mark.

05 · Judgement. What I'd do.

My view:

  • For gold, the fee rise is real money over a year (about ₹55,000 in this example), but it's a fraction of a percent per piece [Arithmetic: ₹4,248 a month is ₹1,062 a week; × 52 weeks = ₹55,224]. Don't let it change your pricing.
  • Rush lots cost more per piece because of the minimum. Batching them helps a little, and only when it doesn't keep a customer waiting.
  • For silver, ask your export buyers directly whether hallmarked 925 would win you orders. If yes, it's cheap proof. If not, it's 1% you don't need to spend yet.

The formula
  • fee_lot = max(n × rate, minimum) × (1 + t)
  • per_piece = fee_lot ÷ n
  • share = per_piece ÷ price
  • monthly = k × fee_lot

What to do this week

  1. Look at last month's hallmarking bills and count how many consignments were under the minimum.
  2. Check which of your pieces are exempt: under 2 g, kundan, polki or jadau, or for export. Confirm your reading with your hallmarking centre.
  3. If you make silver, ask your two biggest buyers whether they'd value BIS-hallmarked 925 with HUID.
