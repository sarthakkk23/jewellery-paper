Question 5 of 8 · Gold stock and cash

"My stock is worth more on paper, but my cash is tighter. What is it really costing me?"

The short answer

Holding stock costs money every month and rides the gold price both ways. Count months, and know which bet you're making.

My view: holding stock does two things at once. It costs money every month it waits, and it rides the gold price, up or down. This year the price paid more than the waiting cost. That won't always be so. What a shop can control is how long each piece waits.

The figure. IBJA 999 gold went from ₹1,20,670 to ₹1,41,710 per 10 g between 6 Nov 2025 and 26 Oct 2026, +17.4% [Measured; Arithmetic]. To hold the same weight of stock, you need 17% more money.

A worked example: a shop holding 1.5 kg of 22K stock

The shop keeps 1.5 kg of finished 22K jewellery on display and in the safe [Example]. Its making is 12% [Example]. Money tied up costs it 9% a year [Estimate: a modest figure. If the shop's real cost of money is higher, every holding cost below is bigger].

Every step of the sum 5 steps

01 · Arithmetic. Same gold, more money.

  • A year ago: 1,500 g × ₹11,053.34 (unrounded) = ₹1,65,80,010 (about ₹1.66 crore).
  • Now: 1,500 g × ₹12,980.6 = ₹1,94,70,900 (about ₹1.95 crore).
  • Extra money tied up in the same 1.5 kg: ₹28,90,890.

02 · Arithmetic. What holding it costs.

  • ₹1,94,70,900 × 9% = ₹17,52,381 a year, or about ₹1.46 lakh a month.
  • That's the cost of money only, before insurance or security.

03 · Arithmetic. One slow design.

  • A 40 g bridal set is worth ₹5,19,224 in gold, and its making at 12% is ₹62,307.
  • Held 3 months, it costs ₹11,683 in interest: 19% of its making.
  • Held 6 months: 38%. Held 12 months: ₹46,730, or 75% of its making.

04 · Arithmetic. The other side: the gold itself moved.

  • Over the same year, the 1.5 kg rose ₹28,90,890 in value (step 01). That's more than the ₹17,52,381 it cost to hold (step 02). In this year, the gold paid for the waiting.
  • The 40 g set, held 12 months: its gold went from ₹4,42,134 to ₹5,19,224, a gain of ₹77,090, against ₹46,730 of holding cost.
  • It works the other way too. Had gold fallen by the same ₹77,090, the set would have cost ₹1,23,820 to keep for the year (₹46,730 + ₹77,090), about twice its making [Arithmetic].
  • Prices do fall. In October 2026 (to the 26th), silver's average was 25% below its January peak, and gold's 5.7% below its May peak [Measured: IBJA monthly averages].

05 · Judgement. One way to look at it.

My view:

  • Two things are going on, and it helps to see them apart. The holding cost (step 03) is there every month, whatever gold does. The price move (step 04) is a bet every shop holding stock is making, whether it means to or not.
  • Sorting stock by months on display, not just by weight, shows where the holding cost is building up. Past six months, a piece has used up more than a third of its making in holding cost.
  • For pieces that have waited a long time, melt-and-remake, exchange offers or a trade sale can cost less than waiting. That depends on what the shop expects gold to do, which nobody knows.
  • A gold metal loan is owed in grams, so what you owe moves with the gold price. That takes some of the price bet off stock you still hold. But if you sell a piece for rupees and repay later, a rise in gold in between raises what you owe. Ask your banker exactly how repayment is priced.
  • These are financing decisions, so take them with your CA and banker.

Rules that matter here (quoted from RBI)

Rules that matter here (quoted from RBI)
  • Gold Metal Loans: under the RBI (Gold Metal Loans) Directions, 2025, effective 1 April 2026, nominated banks may lend gold to jewellers who manufacture or sell, at home or for export. Non-manufacturers "may borrow under GML only for outsourcing their manufacturing" (para 8). For non-exporters the tenor is "subject to a ceiling of 270 days" (para 17). www.rbi.org.in/…/bs_viewcontent.aspx
  • Gold Monetisation Scheme: the government decided to "discontinue the Medium Term and Long Term Government Deposit" components from 26 March 2025; banks "may offer Short Term Bank Deposits" (RBI/2024-25/132, 25 Mar 2025). www.rbi.org.in/…/NotificationUser.aspx
  • Eligibility, pricing and margins are set by each bank. Ask your bank and your CA.

The formula
  • value = w × r
  • carry = value × i × months ÷ 12
  • making_value = m × value
  • share = carry ÷ making_value
  • If the old rate is given: gold_move = w × (r − r_then); net = gold_move − carry

What to do this week

  1. Tag ten pieces with the month they went on display. If you don't know, the stock register or the hallmark date is a good start.
  2. Run the three oldest through the calculator.
  3. Ask your bank one question: do you offer gold metal loans to a shop like mine, and on what terms? Write the answer down and take it to your CA.
