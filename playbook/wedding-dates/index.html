Question 3 of 8 · Wedding dates

"This season has fewer wedding dates. How much stock do I keep, and when?"

The short answer

11 dates instead of 17 can mean half as much again on each one. Stock for the busy days, and keep some money for January.

My view: fewer dates doesn't have to mean fewer weddings. It may mean the same weddings on fewer days. Plan for the busy days, and keep some stock money for January.

The figure [Measured: Drik Panchang, set to Jaipur, read 24 Oct 2026]:

  • Nov–Dec 2026 has 11 wedding dates, against 17 in Nov–Dec 2025: 35% fewer [Arithmetic].
  • Jan–Feb 2027 has 22, against 12 in Jan–Feb 2026: 83% more [Arithmetic].
  • Caveat: published lists differ. Others count between 17 and 22 dates for Nov–Dec 2025, and your customers' pandits may differ again. Treat these as a guide, and use your own panchang in the calculator.

A worked example: bridal sets in an old-city shop

Last Nov–Dec the shop sold 30 bridal sets over 17 wedding dates. Last Jan–Feb it sold 18 over 12 dates [Example].

Every step of the sum 5 steps

01 · Arithmetic. Sales per wedding date, last year.

  • Nov–Dec: 30 ÷ 17 = 1.76 sets per date.
  • Jan–Feb: 18 ÷ 12 = 1.5 sets per date.

02 · Estimate. Two ways the season could go.

  • Weddings fall with dates. Families who can't get a good date in Nov–Dec wait.
  • Weddings crowd onto fewer dates. The same families marry anyway, two or three to a day.
  • The truth is usually in between. The "crowding share" says how much of last year's business turns up anyway: 0 means none, 1 means all of it.

03 · Arithmetic. The forecast.

Forecast = last season × [crowding + (1 − crowding) × this season's dates ÷ last season's dates]

04 · Arithmetic. What doesn't depend on the guess.

  • If the same weddings happen, each Nov–Dec date carries 55% more than last year (2.73 against 1.76 sets).
  • Jan–Feb 2027 has twice as many dates as Nov–Dec 2026 (22 against 11). In the forecast above it sells more than Nov–Dec while the crowding share is below about 0.53; above that, Nov–Dec sells more.
  • The busy days get busier. Stock and staff need to be ready for the days, not just the season.

05 · Judgement. What I'd do.

My view:

  • Have the bridal range ready by the first 2026 date, 21 November.
  • Don't order for the whole of last year's Nov–Dec. Hold back some stock money for January–February 2027, which starts on 15 January (after Kharmas) and has more dates whichever way this season goes.
  • Ask Nov–Dec customers about family weddings in Jan–Feb. Many families have two in a year.

What to do this week

  1. Write down last season's bridal sales and count the dates from the panchang your customers follow.
  2. Mark this season's dates on the shop calendar. On Drik Panchang (Jaipur) they are 21, 24, 25 and 26 November and 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 11 and 12 December. Plan staff for those days first.
  3. Split this season's order: about what the c = 0.5 row suggests now, and a top-up decision on 1 December, once you've seen the first dates.
